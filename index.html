US History 101: Midterm Review Notes

Review materials for the upcoming exam. Focus on the socioeconomic impacts and key legislative documents across all 5 units.

Unit 1: Colonial America

Key Terms to Remember:

  • Jamestown (1607): The first permanent English settlement in North America, funded by the Virginia Company.
  • Mayflower Compact: A set of rules for self-governance established by the English settlers who traveled on the Mayflower.
  • Mercantilism: Economic theory that trade generates wealth and is stimulated by the accumulation of profitable balances.

Unit 2: The American Revolution

Key Terms to Remember:

  • Stamp Act (1765): An act of the British Parliament that exacted revenue from the American colonies by imposing a stamp duty.
  • Declaration of Independence (1776): Document declaring the 13 American Colonies independent from Great Britain.
  • Treaty of Paris (1783): The treaty that officially ended the Revolutionary War and recognized American independence.

Unit 3: The Constitution & Early Republic

Key Terms to Remember:

  • Articles of Confederation: The original weak constitution of the US, replaced by the US Constitution in 1789.
  • The Great Compromise: Agreement providing a dual system of congressional representation to balance large and small states.
  • Bill of Rights: The first ten amendments to the US Constitution, guaranteeing freedoms of speech, assembly, and worship.

Unit 4: The Industrial Revolution

Key Terms to Remember:

  • Textile Industry: The first industry to be industrialized, starting in Great Britain[cite: 22].
  • Assembly Line: A manufacturing process perfected by Henry Ford for mass production[cite: 22].
  • Urbanization: The rapid growth of cities as people moved from rural areas to find factory jobs[cite: 22].

Unit 5: The Civil War & Reconstruction

Key Terms to Remember:

  • Secession: The withdrawal of eleven Southern states from the Union in 1860, leading to the Civil War.
  • Emancipation Proclamation: An executive order issued by Abraham Lincoln freeing slaves in all portions of the United States not then under Union control.
  • Reconstruction Amendments: The 13th, 14th, and 15th Amendments aimed at guaranteeing rights to newly freed slaves.
